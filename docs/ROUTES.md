# Legal routes and the intervention playbook

This is the source for the site's "How to do it" content and for `registry/routes.yaml`. Everything
here was last checked on **2026-10-04** from the research in [research/05](research/05-prior-art-playbook-land-access.md)
and [research/02](research/02-street-safety-transit.md). Rules change: the site shows each entry's
"last checked" date, and entries older than six months are flagged for a recheck. Items marked
**confirm** must be confirmed with the named organization before the site states them as fact.

Placekeepers does not give legal advice. For anything involving ownership, it sends people to
Grounded in Philly's guides and the Garden Justice Legal Initiative's free lawyer request form rather
than rewriting their work.

## 1. Getting permission to use a vacant lot

### City and Land Bank lots

About 26% of Philadelphia's roughly 40,000 vacant lots are publicly owned (City vacant lot program
page). The Land Bank handles most of them (phillylandbank.org; land@phdc.phila.gov; 215-448-3034).

| Route | Who | Terms | Time | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Side or rear yard | The next door owner only | Nominal price; must stay a side yard forever; 30 year mortgage at appraised value that is forgiven if the rules are kept; the City can take it back | Staff aim to respond in 120 days, then Land Bank board and City Council approval. Only 67 parcels were conveyed citywide from 2020 to 2023 | Verified |
| Urban Garden Agreement | Anyone | $1; the City can cancel any time | Short | Verified |
| Individual Garden Agreement | Individuals | $25 a year, one year, liability insurance | Short | Verified |
| Community Garden License | Groups with a nonprofit sponsor | Up to 5 years; insurance, maintenance plan, district council approval | Months | Verified, but **confirm**: Grounded in Philly notes the Land Bank stopped signing new leases in 2020 |
| Interim Use Agreement | Groups | Temporary, tied to a date or event; must follow zoning | Varies | Verified |
| Garden purchase | Gardens operating at least 5 years | Needs the district council member's support | Long | Verified |

The Land Bank runs free webinars on these processes (three were scheduled for October 6 to 8, 2026).
The new Philadelphia Land Steward Union is organizing for a 180 day first refusal window for
neighbors, public acquisition statistics, and faster processing; Placekeepers can supply the
statistics.

Redevelopment Authority lots (1,670): the original project labeled these "do nothing". Placekeepers
says "contact PHDC to ask about this parcel" until the route is researched.

### Private lots

| Route | What it involves | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Ask the owner | A written permission or license agreement. Many owners are reachable at their mailing address. A template letter will be written with legal aid review | Template **to write** |
| Buy it | An ordinary purchase. Often blocked by a tangled title (the owner of record died and the estate was never settled) | Verified |
| Garden adverse possession (2024 law, 53 Pa.C.S. section 1143, effective 2024-09-06) | Philadelphia residents, groups or nonprofits; privately owned land; half an acre at most; 10 years of occupancy, at least 5 as a garden; no permanent structures (fences and sheds allowed); a quiet title lawsuit with a lawyer; existing liens stay | Verified via Grounded in Philly; **confirm** with the Garden Justice Legal Initiative before featuring it |
| General adverse possession | 21 years of open, continuous, exclusive use without permission | Verified |
| Conservatorship (Act 135) | A court appoints a conservator for a property not legally occupied for 12 months, not marketed, not in foreclosure, and not bought in the last 6 months. Since 2014 the petitioner can earn the greatest of $2,500, 20% of costs, or 20% of the sale price. Penn Carey Law researchers found petitions concentrate in gentrifying neighborhoods. No reform enacted 2023 to 2026 | Verified; always shown with the abuse warning in ETHICS.md |

**Sheriff sale risk.** Properties about three or more years behind on taxes, water or City bills can
be sold at sheriff sale (bidding on Bid4Assets). The winning bid clears old liens, so a lot neighbors
have tended for years can be sold to an outside buyer. Placekeepers shows past sheriff sales and links
to Grounded in Philly's sheriff sale guide.

### Lots already maintained

- **PHS LandCare** treats and mows about 12,000 lots under a City contract (cleanup, topsoil, a low
  post and rail fence, trees, regular mowing). These lots are already cared for.
- **Community LandCare** lets residents steward lots with PHS support. Intake process **to confirm**
  (phsonline.org blocked automated access).

### Keeping a garden for good

- **Neighborhood Gardens Trust** (ngtrust.org, 215-988-1630, ngtrust@pennhort.org) preserves
  community gardens permanently. Intake criteria **to confirm**.

### Trees on and along a lot (heat and shade)

Added 2026-10-05 for the heat and shade lens (milestone M3.1). The lot's own permission routes
above come first: planting on a lot, or in the sidewalk in front of it, needs its owner. Both tree
routes were last checked on **2026-10-05** against the programs' own pages.

| Route | What it involves | Cost | Time | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free street trees along the lot (`street_tree_request`, shared with the shade trees at bus stops, section 2) | Street trees go in the sidewalk in front of a property when its owner asks Parks and Recreation's Street Tree Management Division, online, by email at StreetTree.Info@phila.gov, or by phone at 215 685 4363 or 215 685 4362 (weekdays from 7:30 a.m. to 3 p.m.). Neighbors ask the lot's owner, named on the lot page, or the Land Bank for City or Land Bank land. The City inspects the spot, chooses the tree, cuts the concrete and plants it, then waters and mulches it for the first year and replaces a tree that dies within a year. After that, TreePhilly asks for about 20 gallons of water a week from March through December | Free | The next planting season: mid April to mid June, or mid October to mid November | Verified for the program (phila.gov "Get a street tree" and treephilly.org "Street trees", 2026-10-05); **confirm** whether the City plants in front of a vacant lot the City or the Land Bank owns, and who asks for it |
| Free trees from a TreePhilly giveaway (`treephilly_yard_trees`) | For Philadelphia residents, to plant in the ground on private property, never in a container, the sidewalk or the public right of way. Sign up for TreePhilly's newsletter; register with the community group hosting a giveaway, which opens about two weeks to a month before it and closes when the trees run out; pick up the tree (2 to 10 feet tall, in a 5 gallon bucket, with a free bag of mulch). Giveaways run in spring (March to May) and fall (September to December), in priority ZIP codes | Free | The next spring or fall giveaway | **Confirm** whether a giveaway tree may go on a private vacant lot whose owner agrees in writing (source: treephilly.org "Yard trees", 2026-10-05) |

TreePhilly's street tree page also names PHS Tree Tenders as another way to get street trees; PHS's
own pages refuse automated access, so its steps are not described here.

### Helping families keep their homes

- **Tangled Title Fund** (Philadelphia VIP; tangledtitlefund@gmail.com; 215-523-9553): up to $6,500
  toward legal costs of clearing title, plus up to $3,500 toward transfer tax case by case; household
  income up to 80% of area median; the property must be the applicant's home.
- **Fraud Guard** (City Department of Records; 215-686-2290; records.info@phila.gov): a free email
  alert whenever a document naming you is recorded.
- Since 2025-11-10 the City automatically blocks deeds whose seller was already dead when they
  supposedly signed.

**Bus and trolley stops (added 2026-10-05 by M2.3).** A stop's page offers five suggestions, each
with a route in `registry/routes.yaml`: survey the stop (`streetcomplete_survey`), ask for a shelter
(`bus_shelter_request`) or a bench (`stop_bench_request`), report a dark streetlight (`report_to_311`,
then `otis_contact` for a stop with no light at all, since the City's streetlight program replaces
lights but does not add new ones), and plant shade trees (`street_tree_request`, with TreePhilly).
Where no public request path exists, the route says so and names the office that runs the program.
SEPTA's customer service page also offers a safety and incident report that goes to the transit
police; Placekeepers never points there. The OTIS staff contact named in the Art Commission papers
is not published here; the routes use the office's general address.

**Daylighting.** Pennsylvania law (Title 75, section 3353) already bans parking within 20 feet of a
crosswalk at an intersection and within 30 feet of a stop sign or signal. Daylighting a corner
enforces an existing rule.

## 6. Questions to confirm before launch

These go into outreach drafts for the owner to send; agents never send them.

1. OTIS: is there a permit for a bench on a sidewalk? What is the City's position on asphalt and
   crosswalk art after the 2025 federal directive? How can residents request a bus shelter, and will
   the City share the shelter inventory?
2. Land Bank: are garden licenses and leases being issued now?
3. PHS: how does a resident group join Community LandCare? How many lots are in LandCare today?
4. Garden Justice Legal Initiative: how should we present the 2024 garden adverse possession law?
   (Its own sheriff sale guide may predate the law.)
5. Bicycle Coalition and Families for Safe Streets: may we show names from TrafficVictimsPHL, and how
   would families like it done?
6. Mural Arts, the Association for Public Art, philart.net, and Philadelphia's Magic Gardens: may we
   include their artwork locations?
7. Parks and Recreation (Street Tree Management Division) and the Land Bank: will the City plant
   street trees in front of a vacant lot the City or the Land Bank owns, and who should ask?
8. TreePhilly: may a resident plant a giveaway tree on a private vacant lot whose owner agrees in
   writing, or a lot the neighbor holds under a garden agreement?
