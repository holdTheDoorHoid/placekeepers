# Spanish glossary (es)

The choices every Spanish text on the site follows: the interface catalog
(`web/src/i18n/locales/es.ts`), the registry text (`registry/i18n/es.yaml`) and the content pages
(`content/es/`). English is the source. Add a term here before using it in a second place.

## Voice

- Neutral Latin American Spanish that Philadelphia's Puerto Rican, Dominican, Mexican and Central
  American neighbors all read easily. Address the reader as **usted**, never tú.
- Plain, warm, short sentences, the way the English reads. No jargon without a few words of
  explanation.
- No dashes as punctuation (no em dash, no en dash, no spaced hyphen). Ranges are "de 2019 a 2024".
- Percent is written close to the number (`61%`), as in the rest of US Spanish.
- Numbers follow `es-US`: comma for thousands (`1,000`), point for decimals (`0.2`).
- Words with two common forms: **alquiler** (rent, never renta), **inquilino** (renter),
  **cuadra** (a street block), **piscina** (pool), **baño** (restroom), **acera** (sidewalk).

## Names that stay as they are

Placekeepers, Philly311, LandCare (PHS LandCare, Community LandCare), SEPTA, PennDOT, PECO, Fraud
Guard, Tangled Title Fund, Philadelphia VIP, Garden Justice Legal Initiative, Neighborhood Gardens
Trust, Philadelphia Land Bank ("el Land Bank"), PHDC, Free Library, Mural Arts Philadelphia,
OpenStreetMap, StreetComplete, TreePhilly, Clean & Green Philly, Grounded in Philly, Families for
Safe Streets, Mapping Inequality, GitHub.

phila.gov's Spanish pages call the Land Bank "Banco Agrario", a mistranslation, so the name stays
in English. They are inconsistent about the Free Library, so that name stays too.
