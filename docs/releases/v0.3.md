# Placekeepers v0.3

Placekeepers v0.3 finishes Phase 3 of the roadmap, heat and shade and placemaking. A new
placemaking lens ranks vacant lots for where a garden, a place to sit or a mural would be used
most, built on new maps of walking, people and traffic stress for people on bikes. It also brings
forward a displacement watch from the next phase, so that improvements come with ways to protect
the neighbors who live there now, and adds lots the City's land agencies list as available and a
heat map of parking problems reported with Philly Bike Action's Laser Vision app.

**Try it:** https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/

## Highlights

- **Placemaking.** A lens for vacant lots and buildings: many neighbors and everyday places within
  a short walk, far from a park, no public art nearby, on or near a shopping street, each with a
  "why" breakdown. Six suggestions come with it, each with its first lawful step: a place to sit in
  the shade, a community garden, a mural, and reports of dumping, a dark street light or graffiti
  to Philly311. It is about use and welcome, never about crime: no study links these to less
  violence, and the map does not claim it.
- **Walking, cycling and people.** The EPA's walkability index for each of the city's 1,336 block
  groups; how many people and which everyday places lie within a 5 or 10 minute walk of any spot;
  and how stressful each street is for people on bikes, from calm to only for the most fearless
  riders, from the Delaware Valley Regional Planning Commission.
- **Public art.** 651 works as of October 8, 2026, from the City's Percent for Art list,
  OpenStreetMap and Wikidata, each shown once with a link to every source. Memorial artworks show
  without names. (This layer arrived at the very end of v0.2's work, so v0.2's notes did not
  describe it.)
- **Displacement watch.** A layer marking census tracts where public records show signs that prices
  are rising: home sale prices, companies buying homes, the City's assessed values, renters, and
  the City's Market Value Analysis. As of October 8, 2026, 96 of the city's 408 tracts are in it.
  Inside one, every greening suggestion, every placemaking suggestion and every listed lot adds
  the signs and links to protections: the Neighborhood Gardens Trust, community land trusts, the
  Homestead Exemption, the Longtime Owner Occupants Program and help with a tangled title. It is a
  caution, never a ranking, and changes no score.
- **Lots listed as available.** A setting and a filter for the lots the City's land agencies list
  as available, the same list the Philadelphia Land Bank's map shows (1,640 parcels as of October
  8, 2026). The lot page says the City's status in plain words, leads with the side yard program
  where the lot is eligible, and repeats that the Land Bank can say no. No prices, and no ranking by
  how easy a lot would be to get.
- **Parking problems reported.** A heat map of where people using Philly Bike Action's Laser Vision
  app reported vehicles blocking sidewalks, crosswalks, corners, curb ramps and bike lanes, shown
  with Philly Bike Action's permission. It is evidence for fixing the street itself: curb
  extensions, bollards, daylighted corners, protected bike lanes and loading zones. Counts only,
  in areas about a block across, and an area shows only with at least 5 reports (26,060 reports
  in the 12 months to October 7, 2026; 1,112 areas shown).
- **Reachable without the map.** Street blocks and crashes now have lists, like the memorials,
  for people using a keyboard or a screen reader. A lot page opened from a link or a search shows
  its score breakdown, which it did not before. The base map no longer draws labels of artworks,
  memorials, monuments or graves.
- **Said plainly about sources.** Transit Forward Philadelphia's stop audits wait on permission:
  we are waiting for a trimmed copy and a license. PhillyTreeMap is not used: it has been dormant
  since about 2013 and has no data license. Philly Bike Action and the Land Bank are used, with
  credit. Everything checked is in
  [docs/DATA_SOURCES.md](https://github.com/holdTheDoorHoid/placekeepers/blob/main/docs/DATA_SOURCES.md),
  under "Sources checked 2026-10-08."

Heat and shade and amenities, previewed in v0.2, are now part of the finished phase: the City's
libraries, recreation centers, pools and park fountains count as everyday places, and open 311
requests trigger the reports. Full history, historic maps and organizing tools are still ahead.

## Not in this release yet

Names of people killed: we are still asking the Bicycle Coalition and Families for Safe Streets
for their blessing, and removal requests still go through a public GitHub issue until a private
email address exists. Waiting on permission from their owners are philart.net, the Association
for Public Art's map, Philadelphia's Magic Gardens' mosaic map, Parks and Recreation's list of art
in its parks, and Transit Forward Philadelphia's stop audits.

## Known limits

- The placemaking lens values people and everyday places within a walk, so it ranks the dense
  rowhouse neighborhoods around Center City highest, and some of those are among the city's
  costliest. That is why every placemaking card carries a displacement caution, and why a "Far from
  a park" preset turns to places that lack open space.
- The public art factor knows only part of the city's art. Mural Arts' murals are in no list the
  map may use, so it has the lowest weight and the lens says so.
- Walking distances are straight lines, which reach a little farther than a walk along streets and
  cross rails and rivers that people on foot cannot.
- The displacement watch works by census tract, so it cannot see a single block. It shows signs in
  public records, not a forecast, and it cannot tell who has moved away or why, or what rents are.
  93 tracts had too few home sales to judge prices.
- The City's list of available lots carries no dates, so the site dates it by the day we fetched
  it, and the list changes often. A listed parcel our vacancy model does not call vacant is not
  drawn on the map; its lot page still says it is listed. A listing is no promise: the Land Bank
  decides each request on its own.
- Parking reports come from people using one app. They show where those people reported a
  problem, not every problem, and a quiet area is not proof of a safe one.
- Only about 1 in 10 bus and trolley stops are known to OpenStreetMap so far (829 of 7,927 in its
  data of October 3, 2026), so most stop pages say "not yet surveyed" for a shelter or bench.
- No confirmed public way to request a bus shelter yet: OTIS picks stops itself, under its
  advertising contract.
- No filters yet by council district, community organization, neighborhood or zoning.
- Safari on an iPhone has not been tested yet.

## How to help

Add a missing mural or sculpture, or a bench or a bus shelter, to OpenStreetMap; the map reads it
every week. Survey a bus or trolley stop with the free StreetComplete app; see the "Survey bus
stops" guide on the site. Tell us what is wrong or missing on the
[Contact page](https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/contact/) or a
[GitHub issue](https://github.com/holdTheDoorHoid/placekeepers/issues/new/choose).
