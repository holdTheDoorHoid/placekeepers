# Placekeepers v0.4

Placekeepers v0.4 finishes Phase 4 of the roadmap, history and displacement. Every lot page now
tells the story of the lot, with one timeline of its sales, inspections, permits and demolitions,
and a new part, "Rules for this lot," says what the City's records show about historic
designation, zoning, brownfields, appeals and upcoming hearings. A new "Then and now" group of
layers shows the City's aerial photos from 1959 to 2025 on a year slider, the 1860 atlas and the
1937 redlining map. A new page, "The Land Bank in numbers," counts what the City's land agencies
have sold or transferred. The displacement watch gains a sixth sign, rent burden, and a card of
rents, incomes and empty homes, and the map adds the City's bus shelters, street lights, traffic
calming and crossing guards, cooling and warming centers, and playgrounds.

**Try it:** https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/

## Highlights

- **The story of each lot.** Under History, one or two plain sentences built only from records
  ("A building stood here until 2011, when the City demolished it."), each with where it comes
  from, then one timeline, newest first, of deeds, violations, permits, demolitions, clean and
  seal work, unsafe notices, appeals, and the dated lists that called the lot vacant or the year
  PHS LandCare began caring for it. Eight switches turn each kind off and on. It reads the same
  with live City data on or off, and every deed on record back to 1974 is there. No case numbers,
  inspectors, applicants or contractors.
- **Rules for this lot.** Whether the lot is in a historic district or on the Philadelphia
  Register, with the lawful step first: ask the Historical Commission before you change anything,
  including a mural or a garden. The base zoning and each overlay in plain words, with a link to
  the Zoning Code. Federal brownfield records near the address, with "test the soil before growing
  food." Every appeal the City publishes, and a hearing still to come at the top of the page with
  the City's page on how to take part. It never says a lot is or is not buildable. As of October
  9, 2026: 1,655 lots in a historic district or on the Register, 77,290 with zoning overlays,
  3,744 at or near a brownfield property, and 7,427 with appeals, 260 of them with a hearing still
  to come. Five new layers in "Land rules and hearings," all off by default.
- **Then and now.** The City's aerial photographs on a year slider, 23 of them from 1959 to 2025,
  with a setting for how strongly the photo shows. The City's copy of the 1860 Hexamer and Locher
  atlas, for Center City and Northern Liberties. The 1937 redlining map (83 areas), with a plain
  account of what redlining was and a note that the grades say nothing about the
  people who live there today. A button on each lot page, "See this lot in old aerial photos,"
  opens the map at the lot in 1959. The pictures stay on the City's servers, and nothing is asked
  of them until you turn a layer on.
- **The Land Bank in numbers.** A page for the Philadelphia Land Steward Union's campaign and
  anyone else who wants the facts, with no slogans. As of October 9, 2026: 3,235 properties
  sold or transferred by the Land Bank, the Redevelopment Authority, PHDC and the City from
  January 2014 to August 2026, 1,032 of them by the Land Bank. Conveyances per year, who the
  buyers are, side and rear yards (labeled as our inference), the prices deeds record, counts by
  council district, and a weekly count of lots listed as available that starts in October 2026.
  Aggregates only, with a CSV download for each section.
- **Displacement watch.** The watch now also counts rent burden: renters paying half their income
  or more on rent. Like the renters and company buyers signs, it says who is exposed, so an area
  still needs a sign about prices. With it, 104 of the city's 408 census tracts are in the watch, up
  from 96 (as of October 9, 2026). Tapping an area also shows rents, incomes and empty homes from the Census Bureau's
  survey for 2020 to 2024, each with its margin of error and the city's own figure beside it: 29.5%
  of renters pay half their income or more, the middle rent is $1,397, the middle household income
  is $61,953 and 9.2% of homes are empty.
- **Streets and stops.** The City's own list of 487 bus shelters, which the transit comfort lens
  now counts (462 of them stand at 451 of the 7,912 stops it scores; where OpenStreetMap
  disagrees, the stop says so and suggests a survey). The 203,096 street poles the City lists and
  the lamps on them, close in only, always called "poles" and "lamps the City lists," never
  "brightness." The City's 1,780 traffic calming devices, with "No traffic calming recorded here
  yet" on 1,045 High Injury Network blocks where people were hurt. The 758 posts of school
  crossing guards, as a safety service. All off by default.
- **Cooling and warming centers, and playgrounds.** The 86 places the City lists as warming or
  cooling sites, as listed on October 9, 2026 and never live (call first), one marker for each
  place. Parks and Recreation's 462 playgrounds as a layer, and the nearest one on each lot page.
  The owner decided not to add playgrounds to the placemaking score, since it would mostly repeat
  "far from a park."
- **Said plainly about sources.** Three of these additions came from a review of Build Philly
  Now's map on October 9, 2026. Nothing from that map is used: its map, tiles and API are never
  read, and everything comes from the City, the Census Bureau or the EPA directly. City Council's
  resolutions need a token we do not have, and the Land Bank's and PHDC's board documents may not
  be republished, so the program behind most conveyances is not on the page. Everything checked is
  in
  [docs/DATA_SOURCES.md](https://github.com/holdTheDoorHoid/placekeepers/blob/main/docs/DATA_SOURCES.md).

## Not in this release yet

Names of people killed: we are still asking the Bicycle Coalition and Families for Safe Streets
for their blessing, and removal requests still go through a public GitHub issue until a private
email address exists. Waiting on permission from their owners are philart.net, the Association
for Public Art's map, Philadelphia's Magic Gardens' mosaic map, Parks and Recreation's list of art
in its parks, Transit Forward Philadelphia's stop audits, and the Athenaeum of Philadelphia for
the GeoHistory Network's other old maps (the 1875, 1895 and 1910 atlases, the 1942 and 1962 land
use maps, the 1928 photos). Organizing tools are the next phase: printable packets, a neighbor
notice and a log of what was done.

## Known limits

- The old photos are not all alike. The City's were taken from a plane in spring, the photos of
  2000 and earlier (1959, 1975, 1996, 1999 and 2000) are black and white, and the 1975 photos have gaps in the far south, around the
  airport and the Navy Yard. The 1860 atlas covers only Center City and Northern Liberties. If a City picture service is
  down, some pictures may not show; the Data status page says so.
- The 1937 redlining grades say how lenders judged an area then, not anything about the people
  who live there now. The layer is for non commercial use only.
- The Land Bank page counts conveyances from deeds, which do not name the program. Side yards are
  our inference and were checked by hand on only 10 lots. A recorded price is not always money
  paid. The public records do not show how long applicants waited. The weekly count of listed
  lots has no history before October 2026.
- The City's list of bus shelters was last edited in January 2025 and leaves out shelters that
  are not the City's. 25 of its 487 shelters match no SEPTA stop. Only about 1 in 10 stops are
  known to OpenStreetMap so far (829 of 7,927 in its data of October 3, 2026), so most stop pages
  still say "not yet surveyed" for a bench.
- The City's list of poles says what is installed, not what works tonight, and it lists no lamp
  on many poles, such as those PECO owns. Street lights are a layer, not part of any lens.
- Rules for a lot are worked out each week for the lots on our list. A lot outside it links to
  Atlas and the Historical Commission instead, and its appeals still come live from the City. If
  the weekly build lacks a source, the page says it could not check that rule, never that it does
  not apply. Whether a lot is on a corner, and who represents you, are not shown.
- Brownfield records show where a federal grant paid for work. They do not say what is in the
  ground today. Test the soil before you grow food.
- The census numbers on a watch area are survey estimates over five years, and they are rough in a
  small area: half of the tracts' rent burden shares are give or take 14 points or more. The watch
  still works by tract, so it cannot see a single block, and it shows signs in public records, not
  a forecast.
- Cooling and warming sites open and close with the weather. The map shows them as the City listed
  them on the day we copied the list.
- Safari on an iPhone has not been tested yet.

## How to help

Add a missing mural or sculpture, or a bench or a bus shelter, to OpenStreetMap; the map reads it
every week. Survey a bus or trolley stop with the free StreetComplete app; see the "Survey bus
stops" guide on the site. Tell us what is wrong or missing on the
[Contact page](https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/contact/) or a
[GitHub issue](https://github.com/holdTheDoorHoid/placekeepers/issues/new/choose).
