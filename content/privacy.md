# Privacy

Placekeepers is built to need as little of your information as possible.

## No analytics, no cookies, no accounts

Nobody watches how you use this site. There is no analytics, no cookies, no accounts to sign up
for, and no tracking pixels of any kind. There is no Placekeepers server sitting between you and
the site at all; it is simply files published on GitHub Pages.

## Your settings stay in your own browser

The map remembers your choices, like which layers you have turned on, only inside your own
browser's storage, under keys that start with "placekeepers:v1:". That information never leaves
your device and we never see it. Clearing your browser's data will erase it.

## Live lookups to the City

When you open a lot page or search for an address, your browser asks the City of Philadelphia's
own public servers for the newest records: the owner and mailing address, sales, assessments and
inspections, and, for a parcel that is not on our list, counts of what is nearby. These requests go
straight from your device to the City, never through Placekeepers, because there is no Placekeepers
server. The City's servers see them the way they would see a visit to the City's own property
website: they see your internet address and the parcel or address you looked up. Your browser sends
them without cookies.

You can turn this off. Open Settings, and under "Privacy and live data" switch off "Fetch live City
data". Lot pages then show only the weekly copy of the data that Placekeepers publishes, labeled
with its date, and the search box works only with a nine digit parcel number. Your choice is kept
in your own browser and is never put in a link you share, so a link someone sends you cannot turn
it back on.

## Old aerial photos and the 1860 atlas

Under "Then and now" in the layers, you can turn on aerial photos of past years (the City's own,
and older ones it hosts for regional and federal agencies) and the City's copy of the 1860 Hexamer
and Locher atlas. Placekeepers does not copy these pictures. When you turn
one of them on, your browser asks the City's own map service for the pieces of the picture that
cover the part of the map you are looking at. That service runs on Esri's ArcGIS Online, which
hosts the City's maps. Like the live lookups above, it sees your internet address and which part
of the city is on your screen, and your browser sends it no cookies and does not say which site
you came from. Nothing is asked of it until you turn one of these layers on.

These pictures follow the same switch, "Fetch live City data". While it is off, they cannot be
turned on, and a link someone sends you cannot turn them on either.

The 1937 redlining map in the same group is different: Placekeepers publishes it in its own files,
so turning it on asks no other server for anything.
