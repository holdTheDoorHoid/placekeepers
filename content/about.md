# About Placekeepers

Placekeepers is a free map for people who care for their own blocks in Philadelphia. It shows
vacant lots and empty buildings, streets where people have been badly hurt or killed, and the
lawful way to make a place better: start a garden, fix a building, calm traffic, or remember a
neighbor.

## What Placekeepers is not

**It is not a crime map for choosing where to live.** It does not rank neighborhoods by danger,
and it never will. It shows where care would help, not where to stay away from.

**It is not a tool for finding real estate deals.** You will not find an estimated price for
buying a lot here, and you will not find a way to sort lots by how easy they would be to take from
their owner.

**It is not a policing tool.** Placekeepers never suggests calling the police or asking for more
enforcement. Its only suggestion to the City is Philly311, and only for physical problems, like an
open vacant building or a dark streetlight.

**It is not legal advice.** For anything about owning, buying, or gardening on land that is not
yours, read [How to do it](https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/how/) and talk to the
free legal help it points to.

## Who it is for

Placekeepers is built for two kinds of people, often on the same block.

**Neighbors**, standing on the street with a phone, who want to know what they can do about the
empty lot on the corner or the dark stretch of sidewalk by the bus stop.

**Organizers**, planning at a desk, who want to compare many places at once, filter by who owns
what, and find where a little care would reach the most people.

Both kinds of people share the same map and the same facts. A link one person copies opens the
same thing for the other.

## Built on Clean & Green Philly

Placekeepers revives and extends
[Clean & Green Philly](https://github.com/CodeForPhilly/clean-and-green-philly), a project built
by volunteers at [Code for Philly](https://codeforphilly.org/) from 2023 to 2025. It ranked vacant
lots for cleanup and greening because research in Philadelphia found that greening reduces gun
violence nearby. You can read that research on our
[Why this works](https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/why/) page.

Clean & Green Philly closed in July 2025. The City's vacant land list had stopped being reliable
in June 2024, and a volunteer team without funding could not replace it. Placekeepers combines many City records on purpose, so that one broken
source can no longer take the whole map down. Read how on
[How we find vacant land](https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/vacant-land/).

Placekeepers does not reuse any of Clean & Green Philly's code today. It does use the project's
final snapshot of tax debt from July 2025 (MIT license), credited on the
[Data status page](https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/status/). Any of its code we
use later will keep its MIT notice, listed in a NOTICE file in our source code.

## The name

The name comes from placekeeping: caring for a place in a way that helps the people who already
live there, without pushing them out. Every suggestion on this site tries to follow that idea,
including a warning wherever a project might raise nearby prices and rents. Read more on
[Use this responsibly](https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/responsibly/).

## Your privacy

Placekeepers has no accounts, no cookies and no tracking. When you open a lot page or search for an
address, your browser asks the City of Philadelphia's own servers for the newest records, straight
from your device. You can turn that off in Settings. Read how on
[Privacy](https://holdthedoorhoid.github.io/placekeepers/privacy/).

## Open source
