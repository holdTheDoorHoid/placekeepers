# Survey bus stops with StreetComplete

Waiting for a bus is easier with a roof overhead and somewhere to sit. Placekeepers wants to show
which Philadelphia bus and trolley stops have a shelter or a bench, so neighbors and organizers can
see where riders still wait without one. No public list of bus shelters exists, so our map uses
OpenStreetMap, the free map of the world that anyone can improve. OpenStreetMap still knows very
little about the city's stops. You can help fill that gap with a free phone app called
StreetComplete, a few minutes at a time.

[Open the map with the stops that are not yet surveyed](../#l=stop_amenities&s=stop_amenities.show:unsurveyed)

We last checked the app details on this page against StreetComplete's own website, its
[questions and answers page](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/StreetComplete/FAQ) and its
[list of questions](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/StreetComplete/Quests) on October 4, 2026.
StreetComplete is made by volunteers and is not part of Placekeepers, so its screens may change.

## What StreetComplete is

StreetComplete is a free app for adding facts to OpenStreetMap while you are out walking. It looks
for details that are missing near you and shows each one on its map as a small marker, which it
calls a quest. You tap a marker, look around, and answer one simple question, such as "Is there a
shelter at this stop?" Your answer goes straight into OpenStreetMap. You do not need to know
anything about how OpenStreetMap works.

## Install it

- **Android phones.** Install StreetComplete from
  [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=de.westnordost.streetcomplete) or
  [F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/packages/de.westnordost.streetcomplete/). It needs Android 7.1 or
  newer.
- **iPhones.** A first test version for iPhones became available through Apple's TestFlight at the
  end of September 2026. The link is on
  [StreetComplete's project page](https://github.com/streetcomplete/StreetComplete). It is new, so
  expect rough edges.
- **An OpenStreetMap account.** You can start answering right away. To publish your answers, log
  in with a free [OpenStreetMap account](https://www.openstreetmap.org/user/new). Until you do,
  the app keeps your answers on your phone.

The app works offline too. When you are online it sends each answer as soon as you give it, and
when you are not, it keeps the answer and sends it later.

## How the bus stop questions work

1. Open StreetComplete near a stop. Markers appear on its map wherever it has a question.
2. Tap a marker at a bus or trolley stop. The app highlights the stop it is asking about.
3. Look around, then answer. At stops in the United States, StreetComplete asks:
   - Is there a shelter at this stop?
   - Is there a bench at this stop?
   - Is there a waste basket at this stop?
   - Is this stop lit?
   - Does this stop have tactile paving? (the bumpy strip at the curb that helps people who are
     blind find the edge)
   - What number or code identifies this stop? (the stop's number, where its sign shows one)
4. Most questions have a Yes and a No button. If the whole stop is under a roof, such as a deep
   building overhang, tap "Uh…" (the button for other answers) and choose "Whole stop is covered".
   If you cannot tell, tap "Uh…" and choose "Can't say", or leave a note for other mappers. Please
   never guess.
5. If you tap the wrong answer, the undo button in the lower left corner takes it back.

StreetComplete asks about the shelter and the bench again after about four years, so the map can
stay current. In its settings you can turn kinds of questions on or off, for example to show only
the stop questions while you survey. A group can use its team mode, which divides the questions
among up to 12 people mapping the same area at once.

## When a stop is missing from the map

StreetComplete can only ask about stops that are already in OpenStreetMap. On October 4, 2026,
OpenStreetMap had about 830 of the roughly 8,000 bus and trolley stops SEPTA lists in
Philadelphia, so many stops have no questions yet. If you are standing at a stop and the app shows
nothing there, you have two ways to help:

- Switch on StreetComplete's **Things** overlay. It shows bus stops, benches, shelters, waste
  baskets and other things on the street, and lets you add one that is missing where it stands.
  Put a new bus stop on the sidewalk next to the stop's sign.
- Or tap **Create new note** and describe what is missing, so another mapper can add it.

Once a stop is in OpenStreetMap, StreetComplete can ask its questions there too.

## How long a survey takes

These are our estimates, from SEPTA's schedules, not from the app. SEPTA's stops in the city are
about 170 meters apart, a block or two, so walking from one stop to the next takes about two
minutes. Answering the questions at a stop takes about a minute. That makes about three minutes a
stop:

- a stretch of 20 stops takes about an hour;
- one direction of a typical route takes about three hours, because a route has about 55 stops in
  the city each way;
- adding a stop that is missing takes a few minutes more.

Most people survey a stretch near home or along their own ride, and come back another day for the
next one. The other direction of a route has its own stops across the street.

## Stay safe

- Stay on the sidewalk. Never stand in the street or step into a bus lane to look at a stop.
- Stop walking before you look at your phone, and look up before you cross.
- Step aside so riders can wait, board and get off. Never block a bus door or the shelter.
- Survey in daylight, with a friend if you can.
- Do not photograph people. If you add a photo to a note, take it when no one is in the frame.
- Stay on public sidewalks. Do not go onto private property to look at a stop.

## Your answers are public

Every answer you publish is part of OpenStreetMap, under your OpenStreetMap user name, with the
place and the time. Anyone can see it, so choose a user name you are comfortable sharing. What you
add is free for everyone to use under OpenStreetMap's open license, including SEPTA and the City.

## When your answers show up on Placekeepers

StreetComplete sends your answers to OpenStreetMap right away. Placekeepers downloads OpenStreetMap
for Philadelphia every Monday, so your answers reach our map within about a week.

On our map, a stop shows as one of four kinds:

- **A shelter, or the whole stop is under a roof.**
- **A bench, but no shelter mapped.**
- **No shelter and no bench.** Someone checked both.
- **Not yet surveyed.** OpenStreetMap does not say yet, or says only one of the two. This does not
  mean the stop has nothing. It means no one has recorded it yet.

Some mappers draw a shelter or a bench on its own beside the stop instead of answering the stop's
question. Our map counts such a shelter or bench for the nearest stop within 10 meters (about 33
feet), when that stop does not answer the question itself. A stop's own answer always comes first.

## What happens next

As far as we know, bus shelters are placed under the City's advertising contract, and we found no
way for residents to request one. **Not yet confirmed:** ask OTIS directly. See
[How to do it](../how/) for what we know about asking for a shelter or a bench. A clear count of
the stops where riders wait without either is a strong place to start that conversation.
