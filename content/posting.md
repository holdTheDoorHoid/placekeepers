# Where to post a flyer

A flyer on the fence of a vacant lot tells passers by that neighbors care about the place and how
to find each other. Placekeepers can make one for any lot: open the lot's page and choose "Make a
flyer for neighbors". This page is about where you may put it up, lawful places first.

This is not legal advice. We last checked the rules below on October 21, 2026. Rules change, so
check with the City if you are unsure.

## Good places to post

- **Your own window, door or fence.** Your own property is yours to post on. A flyer inside a
  business's window counts toward the City's limit on window signs (signs may cover no more than
  20 percent of the glass without a permit), so a single flyer is fine.
- **The lot owner's fence, with permission.** Ask first. If the owner says yes, write down when and
  how they agreed. The lot's page names the owner as the City publishes it, and the letters page
  has a letter asking an owner for permission to care for the lot.
- **Community boards at libraries and recreation centers.** Ask the staff before you post. The Free
  Library lists putting up materials without advance approval as disruptive, and Parks and
  Recreation allows bulletin and notice boards at its sites. Staff will tell you where and for how
  long.
- **Businesses that agree.** Corner stores, laundromats, barbers and cafes often have a board or a
  window. Ask the owner or manager.
- **Hand it to people.** Handing a flyer to someone who wants it is allowed. Do not leave flyers on
  car windshields or scatter them, and skip any door posted "no flyers".

## Places that are off limits

- **Utility poles, street lights, traffic and parking signs and their posts, historical markers,
  and City trees or street trees.** Philadelphia Code section 10-1202 forbids posting signs on any
  of them. The City fines $300 a sign for a first offense and up to $2,000 a sign after that
  (section 10-1203; the City repeated these amounts in 2018), and charges the cost of removing
  them. Nailing or tacking anything to a utility pole is also a summary offense under Pennsylvania
  law.
- **Trees.** Parks and Recreation's rules forbid fastening any sign to a street tree, its guard or
  its support, or to a park tree, with a fine of at least $300 a day.
- **Car windshields and inside cars.** Philadelphia Code sections 10-711 and 10-723.2 forbid
  putting handbills on or in vehicles; the notice of violation is $100.
- **Mailboxes.** Federal law forbids putting anything without postage in a mailbox.
- **SEPTA shelters, stations and vehicles.** We found no SEPTA rule that allows posting on its
  property, so treat it as off limits and ask SEPTA in writing first.

## Before you print

- **What you print is public.** Anyone can read and photograph a flyer. Prefer a group chat link or
  a separate email address made for the project over a home address or a personal phone number.
- Put a date on it, and take it down when it is out of date.
- The flyer's QR code opens the lot's page on Placekeepers. It carries nothing that tracks who
  scanned it.
