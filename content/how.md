# How to do it

Placekeepers is not legal advice. For anything about owning, buying, or gardening on land that is
not yours, start with [Grounded in Philly](https://groundedinphilly.org)'s guides and the
[Garden Justice Legal Initiative](https://www.pubintlaw.org/cases-and-advocacy/garden-justice-legal-initiative/)'s
free lawyer request form, instead of relying only on this page.

Always try to get permission the lawful way first. It takes longer, but it protects you and it
protects the garden or project you build. This page lists the lawful route first, every time.

We last checked everything on this page on October 4, 2026. Rules like these change, so please
confirm anything important with the organization listed before you rely on it. A few items below
are marked "not yet confirmed." Please do not treat those as settled fact yet; we will update this
page once they are checked.

## Getting permission to use a vacant lot

### Lots owned by the City or the Land Bank

About 26% of Philadelphia's roughly 40,000 vacant lots are publicly owned. The
[Philadelphia Land Bank](https://phillylandbank.org/) (land@phdc.phila.gov, 215 448 3034) handles
most of them.

- **Side or rear yard.** Only the next door neighbor can apply. The price is small, but the lot
  must stay a side yard forever, and the City can take it back if the rules are not kept. The
  Land Bank aims to answer in 120 days, then its board and City Council have to approve it. Only
  67 lots were transferred this way citywide from 2020 to 2023, so expect it to take a while.
- **Urban Garden Agreement.** Open to anyone, for $1. The City can cancel it at any time.
- **Individual Garden Agreement.** For one person, $25 a year, with liability insurance.
- **Community Garden License.** For a group with a nonprofit sponsor, for up to 5 years, with
  insurance and a maintenance plan. **Not yet confirmed:** Grounded in Philly has noted that the
  Land Bank stopped signing new leases of this kind in 2020, so ask the Land Bank directly before
  you plan around one.
- **Interim Use Agreement.** A temporary agreement tied to a date or an event.
- **Buying a garden lot outright.** Open to gardens that have been running for at least 5 years,
  and needs your City Council member's support.

The Land Bank runs free webinars on these programs; three were scheduled for October 6 to 8,
2026. A new group, the Philadelphia Land Steward Union, is organizing for faster, fairer access
for neighbors who already tend City lots.

Lots owned by the Redevelopment Authority (there are 1,670) do not yet have a clear path. For now,
the only step we can suggest is to contact PHDC and ask about the specific parcel.

### Privately owned lots

- **Ask the owner.** Many owners can be reached at the mailing address shown on the lot's page. A
  template letter asking for permission is planned but **not written yet**. Ask the Garden
  Justice Legal Initiative to look over any agreement before you sign it.
- **Buy it.** An ordinary purchase, though many vacant properties have a tangled title (the owner
  died and the estate was never legally settled), which can make a sale difficult.
- **Garden adverse possession.** A 2024 Pennsylvania law lets a Philadelphia resident, group, or
  nonprofit ask a court for title to a small privately owned Philadelphia lot (half an acre or less) after 10 years of use,
  at least 5 of them as a garden, with no permanent buildings. It needs a lawyer and a lawsuit,
  and any existing debts tied to the land stay attached. **Not yet confirmed:** we want the
  Garden Justice Legal Initiative to review how we describe this law before you rely on this
  summary.
- **Ordinary adverse possession.** Pennsylvania's long standing rule: 21 years of open, continuous
  use without the owner's permission can also lead to a court granting title. This is a very long
  path.
- **Conservatorship.** Pennsylvania's Act 135 lets a court appoint a caretaker, called a
  conservator, for a property that has sat empty for a year or more, is not for sale, and was not
  bought in the last 6 months. *Conservatorship can take a property away from its owner.
  Researchers found it is used disproportionately in neighborhoods facing gentrification. Talk to
  the Garden Justice Legal Initiative before you consider it.*

**A risk to know about: sheriff sale.** A property that is about three years or more behind on
taxes or water bills can be sold at a sheriff sale to pay the debt. The winning bidder gets the
property free of those old debts. This means a lot your block has cared for informally, without a
written agreement, could still be sold out from under you. Grounded in Philly has a guide to
sheriff sales.

### Lots already being cared for

**PHS LandCare** already treats and mows about 12,000 vacant lots under a City contract: cleanup,
topsoil, a low fence, trees, and regular mowing. If a lot on the map already shows as LandCare, it
is already being cared for. **Community LandCare** lets a resident group help steward a LandCare
lot. **Not yet confirmed:** how a group signs up is not yet clear to us; contact
[PHS](https://phsonline.org/) directly to ask.

### Keeping a garden safe for good

The **Neighborhood Gardens Trust** ([ngtrust.org](https://ngtrust.org/), 215 988 1630,
ngtrust@pennhort.org) works to permanently protect community gardens from ever being sold out from
under the people who built them. **Not yet confirmed:** exactly which gardens qualify; ask them
directly.

### Help for families keeping their homes

- The **Tangled Title Fund**, run through Philadelphia VIP (tangledtitlefund@gmail.com,
  215 523 9553), pays up to $6,500 toward the legal cost of clearing a tangled title, for
  households earning up to 80% of the area's typical income, for their own home.
- **Fraud Guard**, run by the City's Department of Records (215 686 2290,
  records.info@phila.gov), is a free alert sent whenever a document is recorded with your name on
  it, so you find out fast if someone tries to take your property with a fake deed.
- Since November 10, 2025, the City automatically blocks any deed where the seller was already
  dead on the date they supposedly signed it.

## Street and public space permits

| What you want to do | Who to ask | What it needs | Cost | How long |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm traffic on a residential street (speed humps and similar) | Streets Department | 60% of the block signs a petition | Free to ask | Reviewed three times a year; installed May to October |
| Hold a block party or street event | Streets Department and the police district | 75% of households for a block party, 90% for other events | $25 to $150 | Apply 21 days ahead |
| Run a Playstreet for the summer | Parks and Recreation | 75% petition, a resident supervisor, a small one way street | Free | Summer only |
| Become a Block Captain and run cleanups | Streets Department | 51% of the block signs | Free | Up to three organized cleanups a summer, with tools provided |
| Join Philly Spring Cleanup | Streets Department and PHS | Register | Free brooms, gloves, and bags | Spring |
| Build a parklet | OTIS | Show community support and get insurance | Not stated | Seasonal, April through November; review can take months |
| Ask for a free street tree | Parks and Recreation (TreePhilly) | Request one | Free, if the City plants it | Next planting season |
| Ask for a bike rack | Streets Department | Apply | Not stated | Not stated |
| Paint a wall mural | Mural Arts (apply by March 15 or November 15) | The wall owner's permission | Free to apply | Months |
| Report a dark streetlight, dumping, or an open vacant building | Philly311 | Nothing | Free | Days to weeks |

A few things people often ask about do not have a clear City process yet:

- **A new streetlight.** We found no request path. The City's LED upgrade only swaps bulbs; it
  does not add new lights.
- **A bench on a sidewalk.** **Not yet confirmed:** we could not find a permit path; ask OTIS
  directly.
- **Asphalt or crosswalk art.** We found no City process. In July 2025 the federal government
  began urging cities to remove this kind of street art, and Florida has ordered removals. No
  Pennsylvania or Philadelphia position has been found. **Check with the City first before you
  plan one.**
- **A bus shelter.** **Not yet confirmed:** shelters are placed under the City's advertising
  contract, and we found no resident request path; ask OTIS directly.
- **A roadside memorial or ghost bike.** There is no City rule, but the Bicycle Coalition handles
  ghost bike requests through a simple form, and the norm is to ask the family's blessing first.

**Daylighting a corner** (keeping the area right next to a crosswalk clear of parked cars) is not
a new rule to ask for. Pennsylvania law already bans parking within 20 feet of a crosswalk at an
intersection, so this is about enforcing a rule that already exists.

## Money to help pay for it

- The City's Division of Safe Neighborhoods runs community violence prevention grants: about $24
  million went to 147 groups in June 2025, and a new $25 million round was announced on June 17,
  2026.
- Feet First Philly offers public space mini grants of $500 to $2,000.
- The Block Party Bonanza (a free kit and a gift card) was a 2026 only program and ended on
  October 3, 2026, so it is no longer available.
